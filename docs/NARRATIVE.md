# Narrative notes (generic layout, no business names)

This file is for a game that already has its own plot. It describes only how the real street and land layout can support story and navigation. There are no business names, logos, or copyrighted landmarks here. Any “station” or civic building in the scene is a generic massing, not a replica of a named building.

## The grid

The playable area is a regular American street grid between 17th Street on the north and 28th Street on the south. Numbered streets run east–west; the main north–south spines are, from west to east, the rail-side avenue, a wide central boulevard, and a foothill boulevard before the bench rises. That grid is easy to learn: players can count streets, and a goal can be given as “three streets south and one east” without a map marker.

## The rail edge (west)

The west edge is a hard boundary of rail yards and tracks running north–south, just west of the western avenue. It reads as an industrial seam: ballast, sleepers, and multiple parallel lines. Story uses: a place to arrive or leave the city, a night-time approach with limited crossings, a contrast with the residential grid, or a line that cannot be casually walked across. A generic station-scale volume sits near the tracks where the map records a station — a large, unnamed hall, not a copy of any real depot.

## Downtown core

Around the mid-numbered streets (about 24th–25th) and the central boulevard the blocks tighten: smaller footprints, more commercial massing, parks and civic lots. This is the natural place for crowds, meetings, and “in the middle of town” scenes. Looking east from here, the mountain wall is already obvious above the rooftops.

## The foothill bench (east)

East of the last major boulevard the grid climbs onto a bench of steeper lots and more open ground, then gives way to scrub and rock. Story uses: leaving the city, a climb, a place that looks back over the valley, or the last built streets before wilder terrain.

## The mountain silhouette

East and slightly south of the built-up box the Wasatch front rises more than 1,600 meters above the valley floor (valley ~1,300 m, ridgeline and summit ~2,900 m). The skyline is a constant navigation landmark: if you can see the wall of rock, you know which way is east. Peak forms along the ridge (including a dominant summit just off the southeast corner of the street grid) give a player a way to judge distance and direction from almost any street that looks east. The prototype uses true vertical scale (1×); exaggeration is an optional toggle, not the default.

## River and parks

A river corridor and smaller watercourses cross the grid, with parks and open grass lots at intervals. These are natural beats in a walk: a quieter crossing, a meeting on open ground, or a line of trees that breaks up the blocks. Water is visible and sits on the real alignment from the map, not a decorative afterthought.

## Suggested orientation for play

A default start at 25th Street and the central boulevard, facing east, puts the player in the downtown core, the rail edge at their back, the numbered grid around them, and the mountain wall on the horizon. That one view explains the whole map.
